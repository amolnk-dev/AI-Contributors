Here's an analysis of the Norse civilization simulator competition results for Round 7, Seed 0:

**1. WHERE are the errors concentrated spatially?**
The KL Error map (bottom-right) shows a fairly uniform light cyan color across the entire map, with subtle patches of darker cyan/blue. This indicates that while errors are widespread, they are not intensely concentrated in a single bright red hot-spot. However, by comparing the **Prediction** to the **Ground Truth**, we can infer the primary areas where the model is wrong:
*   **Bottom-left quadrant:** The Ground Truth shows a large, dense cluster of settlements and at least one port here, which is almost entirely absent in the Prediction. This is a major area of missed growth.
*   **Top-middle and right-middle areas:** The Ground Truth displays significant horizontal bands and clusters of settlements and ports along what appears to be a coastline or river system, which are largely under-predicted or completely missed by the model.
*   **Central area (around the initial brown mountains):** The Ground Truth shows a significant expansion of settlements around this region, which the model largely fails to capture, predicting scattered, smaller settlements instead.
*   In summary, errors are concentrated in regions where the Ground Truth demonstrates substantial **settlement expansion and port development**, particularly in areas that were initially empty or forested.

**2. WHAT types of terrain transitions is the model getting wrong?**
Based on the per-class KL error breakdown and visual comparison:
*   **Missing Settlement Growth (KL=0.0750 - highest error):** This is the predominant error. The model significantly under-predicts the growth and clustering of settlements. In the Ground Truth, many light blue (empty) and green (forest) squares transition to orange (settlement), forming large, often interconnected urban areas. The model largely fails to predict these large-scale transformations, retaining much of the empty and forest terrain.
*   **Missing Port Development (KL=0.0581 - second highest error):** The model fails to predict the emergence of red ports. Ground Truth shows several ports, especially along implicit coastlines (adjacent to light blue), but the Prediction shows very few, if any, and none in the locations where they actually appear.
*   **Incorrect retention of Empty/Forest (KL for Empty=0.0390, Forest=0.0284):** As a direct consequence of missing settlement and port growth, the model incorrectly keeps many squares as light blue (empty) or green (forest) that should have transitioned to orange (settlement) or red (port).
*   **Correctly Predicted:** The model performs very well on **Mountain** (KL=0.0000) terrain, predicting no changes, which is accurate. It also performs well on **Ruin** (KL=0.0048), suggesting that if a settlement does collapse, the model generally predicts its ruin status correctly (though there are very few ruins in GT overall).

**3. Are there PATTERNS in the error heatmap?**
The KL Error heatmap is predominantly a uniform light cyan with only very subtle darker cyan/blue variations. There are no prominent "red" areas or distinct visual patterns like rings around specific features, clear linear coastal strips of error, or highly localized error clusters.
The *absence* of strong, distinct patterns suggests that the model isn't making specific, systematic *local* errors (e.g., mispredicting the exact boundary of a predicted settlement). Instead, the diffuse nature of the cyan/blue error, coupled with the high per-class KL for settlements and ports, implies a more global failure to capture the overall *magnitude* and *distribution* of **growth and change**. The error is widespread because the model broadly underestimates the dynamism of the simulation, particularly the expansion of civilization.

**4. What specific improvements would reduce the largest errors?**
The largest errors stem from the model's inability to predict large-scale settlement and port growth. To reduce these:
*   **Improve Modeling of Settlement Expansion Dynamics:**
    *   **Incorporate spatial propagation rules:** The model needs to understand how settlements grow into adjacent empty or forest tiles, forming clusters. This might involve convolutional filters with a larger receptive field or graph neural networks to capture neighborhood interactions.
    *   **Model population/resource dynamics:** If the simulation involves underlying population or resource growth, the model needs to be informed by these factors to drive settlement expansion.
    *   **Longer-term temporal reasoning:** The model needs to capture changes over 50 years, not just immediate transitions.
*   **Enhance Port Prediction Logic:**
    *   **Stronger coastal feature recognition:** The model must accurately identify "coastal" tiles (empty tiles adjacent to other terrain types that could represent water).
    *   **Combine coastal proximity with settlement presence:** A port is a *coastal settlement*. The model needs to learn this combined condition for development.
*   **Address Under-prediction Bias:** The model appears conservative, under-predicting positive changes (growth). Tuning the model's output thresholds or its loss function to penalize false negatives (missing actual settlements/ports) more heavily could help.
*   **Feature Engineering/Attention for Growth Regions:** If there are underlying features that drive rapid growth (e.g., fertile land, strategic locations), the model should be able to identify and prioritize these areas for settlement/port development.

**5. Compare the prediction and GT — what does the model "understand" well vs poorly?**

*   **What the model understands well:**
    *   **Environmental Stability:** The model has an excellent understanding of static environmental features. It accurately predicts that **Mountains** will not change (KL=0.0000). It also generally preserves **Forests** where settlement growth doesn't occur.
    *   **Initial Conditions:** The model successfully processes the initial map state, keeping existing features stable unless specific growth rules are met.
    *   **Ruin Mechanics (Limited Context):** The very low KL for Ruin suggests that if the model predicts a settlement, it can accurately predict its decay to a ruin when applicable, although this is a rare event in this specific ground truth.

*   **What the model understands poorly:**
    *   **Civilization Dynamics and Growth:** This is the model's biggest weakness. It fundamentally fails to capture the core dynamic of the simulation: the significant **growth and expansion of a Norse civilization** over 50 years. It does not understand:
        *   **How settlements cluster and expand:** It largely predicts individual, scattered settlements rather than dense urban sprawl.
        *   **The conditions for port development:** It misses almost all instances of settlements transitioning to ports.
        *   **The overall emergence of macro-level patterns:** The Ground Truth shows clear patterns of civilization spreading and developing, which the model largely misses, resulting in a much more static and underdeveloped map.
    *   **Interaction between terrain types leading to change:** The model struggles to understand how "empty" or "forest" terrain becomes "settlement" or "port" based on the simulation's rules for development and expansion.
